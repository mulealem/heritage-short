# Story brief — “Eat, My Clothes, Eat!” (ብሉ ልብሶቼ ብሉ)

The one page that comes before a storyboard: the story in a few sentences,
one picture per beat, who it is for, and the one feeling it should leave.
`pipeline/script.py` can draft a storyboard from this file (or you can
hand-write `storyboard.json` against the schema, which is what happened here).

> This file was written back from the locked storyboard after the film was
> made, so the brief is on record and the script stage has its input. The
> storyboard itself was authored by hand, beat by beat.

## The story, in five sentences

A wedding feast is in full swing. Aleqa Gebre Hanna arrives straight from the
road, dusty and patched, and the host's gatekeeper turns him away as unfit for
honourable guests. He does not argue; he goes home, washes, and dresses in his
finest white shamma and velvet kabba. He returns, and the same gatekeeper bows
to the ground while the host seats him above everyone. Then, in front of the
whole tent, he feeds the feast to his clothes: “Eat, my clothes, eat! You
invited my clothes, not me.”

## Who it is for

Habesha households — families who grew up on Gebre Hanna stories, plus anyone
who loves a sharp folk-tale sting. Many of them already know the punchline;
the pleasure is in the re-telling.

## Where and how it is watched

On a phone, vertical, shared in family group chats — so faces must read at
thumbnail size, the line must land even with the sound off (English captions),
and nothing may depend on a detail smaller than a thumb.

## The feeling at the end

A laugh with a sting: everyone knows a gatekeeper who judges cloth before
character. They should leave quoting the line, and the proverb with it:
*judge a person by their character, not their cloth.*

## Language

Spoken in Amharic, the language the tale is told in at home. Subtitles and
on-screen captions in English, so the diaspora children and the non-Amharic
viewer follow every line. The proverb appears in both at the end.

## Constraints

- 60–90 seconds, ten beats of six to sixteen seconds, 9:16 and 16:9.
- Two named characters, one place. Fewer characters, fewer consistency
  problems.
- A folk tale in the public domain; no living person's face or voice.

## What is deliberately not in it

No narrator on screen, no violence, no modern objects. The humour is entirely
in the reversal: the clothes get the welcome, so the clothes get the food.
